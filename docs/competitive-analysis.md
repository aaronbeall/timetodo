# Competitive analysis

Status: Working draft  
Market facts last checked: 2026-08-24

## Executive conclusion

Daily planning is crowded, and circular schedule visualization is already a recognizable subcategory. The opportunity is not to claim invention of the radial day. It is to become the best planner for **seeing and repairing a changing day**.

The closest competitor, Dialed, materially overlaps the current concept: radial tasks, overlap layers, direct dragging, progress, recurrence, analytics, calendar import, and themes. Time To Do needs to differentiate through the complete behavior of the product—especially occurrence-safe edits, fast recovery actions, legibility, cross-day rings, and privacy—not merely through circular presentation.

The launch opportunity is narrower and more credible than “the circular planner”: **an Android-first, task-native radial planner designed around time blindness and schedule recovery**. Android has strong visual planners, but the closest task-native radial products remain concentrated on iOS. This gap is time-sensitive rather than permanent.

## Market patterns

### What users can already buy

- Circular calendars that show existing events.
- Visual timelines that combine tasks and calendar events.
- ADHD-oriented planners emphasizing time blindness and routines.
- Premium daily-planning rituals for professionals.
- AI schedulers that automatically rearrange tasks.

### Features moving toward table stakes

- Calendar import and multiple-calendar support.
- Recurring tasks with one-off exceptions.
- Notifications and reminders.
- Home-screen and lock-screen widgets.
- Cross-device sync or dependable backup.
- A watch or glanceable current-task surface.
- Dragging and resizing time blocks.

### Areas that remain weakly owned

- A day that feels like one finite visual object rather than a scrolling feed.
- Recovery after interruption: “what should move now?” without a planning ceremony.
- Direct manipulation that remains precise and accessible.
- Recurrence that clearly separates today's exception from the future series.
- A coherent radial language spanning today, navigation, history, and calendar.
- Local-first privacy presented as part of calmness rather than as a technical footnote.

## Android launch wedge

Android is not an uncontested market. Sectograph is a mature radial calendar with more than five million reported downloads, and Structured and Tiimo both have meaningful Android products. The whitespace is the intersection of four attributes:

1. Android-first product quality and distribution.
2. Task-native radial planning rather than calendar visualization.
3. Lived-experience credibility around ADHD and time blindness.
4. Direct schedule recovery after plans slip.

This wedge can be summarized internally as:

> **The malleable radial day planner for time-blind Android users.**

“Android-first” should mean more than being downloadable from Google Play. The product should eventually exploit Android's strongest glanceable and actionable surfaces: responsive home-screen widgets, notification actions, Wear OS, system-calendar integration, dynamic color where appropriate, and dependable background reminders.

The absence of Dialed and Weel from Android is an advantage now, not a moat. Weel is collecting Android interest, and a cross-platform implementation is always possible. Speed matters, but a rushed launch that fails at reminders, accessibility, or data durability would waste the opening.

## Strategic positioning map

Two useful axes are **user-directed ↔ automatically scheduled** and **linear/list ↔ spatial/whole-day**.

- Motion sits toward automatic and linear.
- Sunsama and Structured sit toward user-directed and linear.
- Sectograph sits toward spatial but is primarily passive/calendar-driven.
- Dialed, DayDial, Weel, and DayByDay sit closest to user-directed and spatial.
- Time To Do should occupy the far user-directed/spatial corner, with schedule recovery as its defining behavior and Android as its initial distribution wedge.

## Competitive implications

### Do not compete on feature count

Structured, Tiimo, and established calendar products have broader ecosystems. Matching them checkbox for checkbox would dilute the interaction that makes this product special.

### Do not market “the circular planner” as the entire novelty

That claim is easy to disprove and does not explain why the app is better. The circle is the mechanism; understanding and reshaping the day is the outcome.

### Build the missing adoption bridge

Calendar import, reliable reminders, widgets, and backup are not the brand, but they determine whether users can live in the product. These should preserve the task-native editing model rather than turn the app into another calendar client.

### Make the interaction visible in marketing

A static screenshot can make radial planners look interchangeable. A short motion demonstration of dragging, resizing, and changing only today's occurrence can communicate the difference quickly.

## Recommended competitive claim

> The radial planner built for changing the plan—not merely displaying it.

Use this as an internal strategic line unless and until it tests well as customer-facing copy.

## Research gaps

- Conduct hands-on trials of Dialed, DayDial, Weel, DayByDay, Structured, and Sectograph rather than relying on listings alone.
- Collect and code recent negative reviews for unmet needs, confusion, and willingness-to-pay signals.
- Maintain an Android-specific watchlist, especially Weel's signup/release status and Dialed's platform plans.
- Compare Sectograph's Android widgets and Wear OS behavior with the intended task-native experience.
- Track how direct competitors handle calendar conflicts, one-off recurrence changes, and rescheduling after missed tasks.
- Validate whether users understand the polar display without a tutorial.
