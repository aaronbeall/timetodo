# Time To Do strategy docs

Status: Working set  
Last reviewed: 2026-08-24

These documents are the working source of truth for the product's strategy, market position, business model, and identity. They are meant to be edited in place as decisions change—not extended as a chronological research stream. Git provides the history.

## Documents

- [Product thesis](product-thesis.md): what the product is, who it serves, why it should win, and the principles that protect its value.
- [Competitive analysis](competitive-analysis.md): direct and adjacent competitors, market patterns, pricing, whitespace, and strategic implications.
- [Positioning](positioning.md): category, audience, promise, differentiators, proof, and messaging hierarchy.
- [Marketing](marketing.md): launch story, acquisition channels, store presentation, activation, metrics, and experiments.
- [Monetization](monetization.md): business-model recommendation, free/paid boundaries, pricing, and validation plan.
- [Branding](branding.md): naming directions, verbal identity, logo concept, color, typography, and visual language.
- [Product roadmap](product-roadmap.md): market-facing priorities and the capabilities needed to support the intended position.

## Current working direction

The product is a visual daily planner that turns time into space. Its polar clock shows the shape of a day at a glance, while direct manipulation makes the schedule easy to repair when reality changes.

The active working brand direction is:

> **Diyal**  
> Visual planner for time-blind brains  
> **See time. Shape the day.**

The initial market wedge is an Android-first, task-native radial planner designed around ADHD/time blindness and schedule recovery. Diyal is an active candidate, not a cleared trademark; it needs pronunciation testing and formal trademark, App Store, domain, and social-handle diligence before adoption.

## How to maintain these docs

- Lead with the current conclusion. Remove or rewrite stale recommendations instead of preserving every iteration.
- Date facts that can change, especially pricing, platform support, rankings, and download counts.
- Label uncertain ideas as hypotheses and identify how they could be tested.
- Keep evidence and source links in the document where a decision depends on them.
- Treat implementation details as evidence, not permanent commitments. Update them when the product changes.
- Put engineering specifications and task tracking elsewhere; these files explain why and what, not ticket-level implementation.
