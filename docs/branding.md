# Branding

Status: Working draft  
Last reviewed: 2026-08-24

## Brand idea

Time becomes visible, tangible, and adjustable.

The brand should combine the calm of a well-shaped day with the active satisfaction of moving pieces into place. It should feel modern and precise, but not clinical; playful enough to be memorable, but not like a novelty clock.

## Active naming direction

### Name

**Diyal**

Pronunciation: **DYE-uhl**, like “dial.”

### Descriptor

**Visual planner for time-blind brains**

### Tagline

**See time. Shape the day.**

### Hero line

> Diyal turns today's tasks into a malleable map. See the whole day as arcs, then move it as life moves.

“Diyal” is an invented compression of **day + dial**. It also contains a useful secondary reading—**DIY your day**—that supports direct control and malleability without requiring literal clock imagery. It is short, sharp, and capable of carrying an abstract identity.

The principal weakness is spelling: someone hearing the name may write “dial,” and someone seeing it may initially say “dee-yahl.” The descriptor and launch motion can teach the pronunciation, but this must be tested rather than assumed.

This is not a legally cleared name. Initial web/store searches did not surface a direct planner named Diyal, but the word exists as a surname and place name. Formal clearance must include relevant trademark classes, App Store and Play Store listings, domains, company registries, and social handles in intended markets.

## Naming directions

### Direction A: day + dial/radius constructions

Current focus. The goal is a short invented word that carries the spatial-day concept without looking like a generic clock utility.

- **Diyal** — strongest current candidate; day + dial, with a useful DIY/control undertone. Requires pronunciation and spelling testing.
- **Raday** — radius + day; relatively compact, but has ambiguous “rah-day/ray-day” pronunciation and little immediate meaning.
- **Dayl** — extremely minimal, but likely to be heard as the name “Dale” and visually offers too little semantic help.
- **Arcaday** — arc + a day; memorable wordplay, but easily heard as “arcade,” which may pull the brand toward gaming.
- **Dyal** — an old spelling related to dial and visually sharp, but loses the explicit day construction and collides with surnames.

Names screened out during initial exploration:

- **DayDial** — occupied by a directly relevant radial day planner at [daydial.app](https://daydial.app/) and by other clock/calendar products.
- **DayArc** — currently used by multiple apps across journaling, routines, weather, and daily planning.
- **Dayra** — used by multiple apps and an established fintech company.
- **Radii** — used by current mobile apps and companies; also a generic geometry term.

### Direction B: short and contemplative

Best when building a distinctive lifestyle brand, but requires a permanent descriptor.

- **Nunc** — Latin for “now”; concise and conceptually relevant, though pronunciation and searchability need testing.
- **Mā** — a Japanese concept often associated with interval or negative space; visually elegant but difficult to search, type, and own.
- **Koyomi** — relates to calendar/time reckoning in Japanese; lyrical, but longer and culturally specific.

Avoid borrowing “Zen” or Japanese concepts as surface decoration. If such a name is used, its meaning should be accurate, respectfully applied, and secondary to clear product explanation.

### Existing name: Time To Do

The working name is clear but generic, hard to own in search, and easy to confuse with ordinary copy. It may remain useful as a project name or literal phrase but is weaker as a durable consumer brand.

### Previous option: Todo'clock

Keep as a historical option, not the recommendation. It is understandable and playful, but leans heavily on generic clock imagery, feels more whimsical than the desired sharp identity, and has an existing completed productivity project called [ToDo o'clock](https://ms.techlabs.org/projects/it-s-todo-o-clock). The punctuation also creates avoidable spelling and handle friction.

## Name evaluation criteria

Score finalists on:

- Spoken recall after one exposure.
- Ability to spell after hearing it.
- App Store and web search distinctiveness.
- Trademark and domain viability.
- Fit with both calmness and active editing.
- Ability to travel across languages and platforms.
- Compatibility with a compact app icon.
- Room to grow beyond a single circular visualization without becoming misleading.

## Logo concept

The logo should be built from **task arcs, displacement, and negative space**, not from a clock face.

- **Task arcs:** two or three substantial curved bands, clearly read as movable pieces rather than a complete ring.
- **Displacement:** a hard-edged offset copy shows where an arc was or could move, turning the flat shadow into a symbol of rescheduling.
- **Negative space:** the missing center and asymmetric gaps keep the mark open, active, and distinct from a clock.

The flat shadow should not imitate light. It is a graphic “ghost position” of the plan: the main arc is now; the offset arc is the previous or possible placement. This gives the stylistic treatment product meaning.

The mark should remain recognizable at 16–24 px and work with one ink. In digital use, the shadow may be a tint of the same monochrome hue; in true one-color reproduction, it can be created with knockout/overlap rather than a second color.

### Construction guidelines

- Never use a complete outer ring, central hands, hour ticks, numerals, or a checkmark.
- Prefer two or three asymmetric arc segments over concentric decorative rings.
- Use sharply cut or slightly angled terminals rather than soft pill-shaped ends.
- Offset the shadow consistently down-right or slightly clockwise; do not blur it.
- Avoid marks that read primarily as a loading spinner, wireless signal, sunrise, person, or Apple Activity rings.
- Explore a subtle `D` or `Y` through the gaps only if it emerges naturally from the arc construction.
- Prepare icon, horizontal lockup, wordmark-only, and monochrome forms.
- Test against the crowded visual field of productivity app icons.

## Color system

### Foundation

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary ink | Obsidian | `#111116` | Text, dark surfaces, monochrome mark |
| Primary light | Warm Rice | `#F5F1E8` | Light background; warmer and calmer than white |
| Signature accent | Citron | `#C7F45B` | Optional UI accent; not required in the monochrome brand mark |

### Task colors

| Name | Hex | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task Teal | `#2FD4C5` | Clear, calm, fresh |
| Task Coral | `#FF6B64` | Human, urgent without pure red |
| Task Periwinkle | `#6E7CF6` | Focused, digital, cool |
| Task Amber | `#F6B44B` | Warm, energetic, visible |

Use Obsidian and Warm Rice for most branded surfaces. The primary identity and logo are monochrome; task colors belong to the product's information layer rather than the mark. Citron remains an optional UI cue, not a required brand signature. Confirm WCAG contrast for every foreground/background pair and provide non-color state cues.

## Typography

- Use a contemporary grotesk with friendly geometry, such as Manrope or Geist, subject to license and platform testing.
- Test lowercase `diyal` against uppercase `DIYAL`. Lowercase is calmer; uppercase exposes the useful DIY reading and feels sharper.
- Use tabular numerals for time readouts.
- Keep headlines short and spatially composed; avoid overly thin weights that compete with task arcs.
- Typography should be more restrained than the name, balancing its wit.

## Visual language

- Thick arcs, sharply cut endpoints, and large areas of negative space.
- Cropped orbits that continue beyond the frame, suggesting a day larger than one screen.
- Monochrome flat color with sharp contrast and hard, unblurred offset shadows; avoid gradients, glow, glass, and simulated depth.
- Small task ticks used as punctuation, bullets, dividers, and selection states.
- Ring-based compositions should encode something when possible rather than serve as empty decoration.
- Photography, if used, should show real transitions and imperfect days rather than aspirational desk still lifes.

## Motion language

- Arcs slide and resize along their actual path.
- The hard shadow can remain momentarily in the prior position as the main arc moves, then snap into alignment.
- Completed tasks resolve with a subtle hand-to-check motion rather than confetti.
- Changes should feel springy but controlled, with a reduced-motion alternative.
- Brand animation should teach the product's spatial model whenever possible.

## Voice

The voice is concise, perceptive, calm, and lightly witty.

Use:

- “That took longer. Move what comes next.”
- “Change just today.”
- “There’s room at 3:30.”
- “Your day, in the round.”

Avoid:

- “Crush your goals.”
- “You failed your streak.”
- “Optimize every minute.”
- Vague spiritual claims or forced clock puns in every message.

## Clearance and validation checklist

- Run knockout and professional trademark searches in launch markets.
- Search Apple and Google app stores for exact, phonetic, and visually similar names.
- Check primary domain variants and major social handles.
- Test pronunciation, spelling, recall, and emotional tone with target users.
- Test the icon in real home-screen grids at small sizes and in monochrome.
- Confirm that the apostrophe renders consistently in URLs, metadata, and voice search.

## Open questions

- Does Diyal reliably produce the intended “dial” pronunciation and day/dial association?
- Does the DIY undertone feel empowering or accidental?
- Should the consumer brand retain a subtle link to Time To Do during transition?
- Which arc construction avoids reading as a loading spinner while staying legible at Android launcher size?
- Should the flat shadow be a linear offset or a slight clockwise “previous position” rotation?
- Which name retains meaning if future views extend beyond the circular day?
