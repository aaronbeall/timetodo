# Marketing

Status: Working draft  
Last reviewed: 2026-08-24

## Marketing objective

Make the interaction legible in seconds, attract people who already feel underserved by lists and calendar grids, and prove that the product becomes more valuable when a plan changes.

The circle will earn attention. The movement of tasks and the clarity it creates must earn installation and retention.

## Launch narrative

### Problem

A list tells you what to do. A calendar tells you when things happen. Neither necessarily helps you feel the whole day or recover when it stops going to plan.

### Product reveal

Time To Do maps tasks around a clock. Duration becomes visible, overlap is obvious, and the available day has a shape.

### Payoff

Drag a task later, stretch its duration, start it now, or change only today's occurrence. The plan changes as quickly as life does.

## Creative rule: motion first

The core concept is materially stronger in motion than in static screenshots. The primary demo should take about seven seconds:

1. Show a full but readable day ring.
2. Reveal a conflict or a late-running task.
3. Drag one arc later and resize another.
4. Trigger Do Now or Snooze.
5. Pull back to a month of miniature day rings.

The same motion can become the App Store preview, landing-page hero, social loop, onboarding sample, and press asset.

## App Store presentation

Recommended screenshot sequence:

1. **See your whole day.**
2. **Move plans by touch.**
3. **Change today. Keep the routine.**
4. **Know what overlaps—and what fits.**
5. **Your schedule stays on your device.**

Use captions to explain the outcome, not label UI controls. The imagery should show the same recognizable day progressing through the sequence.

Candidate search language to validate:

- visual day planner
- radial calendar
- time blocking
- ADHD planner
- time blindness
- daily schedule
- visual to-do list
- routine planner

Do not over-repeat keywords in customer-facing prose; validate store volume and competition before final metadata.

## Channel priorities

### 1. Product-led short video

Publish concise interaction clips on TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, and product-design communities. Demonstrate a recognizable schedule problem rather than only rotating the clock as eye candy.

Example hooks:

- “My brain never understood calendar grids, so I made the day a clock.”
- “What happens to your plan when the 10 a.m. task takes an extra hour?”
- “Change today's routine without changing every Tuesday.”

### 2. Design and indie-launch communities

The product has a visually distinctive story suitable for Product Hunt, Hacker News/Show HN when technically relevant, Indie Hackers, relevant Reddit communities, and app-design publications. Tailor the story to each community; avoid drive-by promotion.

### 3. ADHD and time-blindness education

Create practical content about visual time, estimating duration, and recovering a disrupted day. Collaborate with creators or coaches only when the claims are concrete and they have genuine editorial freedom. Position the product as ADHD-friendly, not as treatment.

### 4. App Store discovery

Invest in screenshots, preview video, localization, ratings prompts after a meaningful success, and keyword testing. Store conversion is likely to matter more than a broad brand campaign at launch.

### 5. Referral through visible output

Explore privacy-safe shareable day summaries or visual plans. A distinctive ring can carry the brand organically, but sharing must be opt-in and make schedule redaction easy.

## Activation journey

The first session should reach the “spatial aha” before asking for extensive setup.

1. Present a sample day or create the first timed task quickly.
2. Prompt the user to drag or resize an arc.
3. Show the precise list/timeline as a companion, not a fallback.
4. Demonstrate a one-day change to a recurring task.
5. Offer calendar import only after the interaction is understood, unless research shows import is the dominant entry path.

Candidate activation events:

- First timed task created.
- First arc moved or resized.
- First quick recovery action used.
- First occurrence-only recurrence edit.
- Return on the next planned day.

The best north-star candidate is **days actively shaped per week**: a day counts when the user both views a schedule and creates, moves, resizes, or resolves a task. This better reflects product value than raw task count.

## Measurement

### Acquisition

- Store-page conversion by creative and keyword.
- Cost per install by audience, if paid acquisition is tested.
- Organic installs attributable to shared visuals and creator content.

### Activation

- Percentage reaching the first arc interaction.
- Time to first spatial edit.
- Percentage planning a second day.

### Retention

- Day 1, Day 7, and Day 30 retention.
- Active days shaped per week.
- Percentage using a recovery action during a changed day.
- Retention difference between calendar importers and task-native users.

### Revenue

- Trial start and trial-to-paid conversion.
- Annual vs. lifetime selection.
- Paid conversion after widgets, calendar integration, or advanced recurrence is encountered.

Analytics should be privacy-respecting, minimal, disclosed, and compatible with the local-first promise. If no analytics are added, use voluntary beta interviews and privacy-preserving aggregate store metrics.

## Initial experiments

| Hypothesis | Test | Success signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motion explains the product better than a static hero | Compare store/landing variants | Higher visit-to-install or waitlist conversion |
| “Plans that change” is stronger than generic visual planning | Test two hero messages | Higher qualified click-through and recall |
| The polar interaction creates activation | Compare guided drag vs. unguided first run | More second-day returns after first drag |
| A broad visual-planner identity outperforms an ADHD-first identity | Segment ads or landing pages without changing the product claim | Better retained users per acquisition dollar |
| Privacy contributes to trust | Add or remove local-first supporting copy | Improved conversion or interview preference without confusing the core story |

## Asset backlog

- Motion logo and seven-second hero loop.
- App Store screenshot system and preview video.
- Landing page with interactive or recorded arc movement.
- Press kit: icon, wordmark, screenshots, short/long descriptions, founder story.
- Short demo library showing overlap, snooze, Do Now, recurrence exception, and month rings.
- Accessible alt text and reduced-motion versions for every core creative.

## Open questions

- Which audience produces retained use rather than curiosity installs?
- Can the core interaction be understood without voiceover or tutorial text?
- Does showing a densely scheduled day create clarity or anxiety?
- What moment naturally earns a rating or referral request?
- Is a shared radial day useful enough to become an acquisition loop without exposing private information?
